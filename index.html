Process, stated generically. Diode light pumps a ytterbium-doped fiber. The industrial beam is near-infrared, about a 1 micrometer class, and it is invisible. A passive fiber delivers it to a head that collimates and focuses it. Assist gas leaves the nozzle around the beam and expels molten metal. Inert gas only clears the melt (fusion cutting). Oxygen can react with hot carbon steel and add heat (reactive fusion cutting). Air is used on some jobs. Which gas fits is a setup choice. This page specifies none.

Cut marks you can name without measuring them: kerf, taper, striations on the cut face, dross where melt resolidified, and a heat-affected zone beside the kerf. Nothing here is a tolerance, a feed rate, or a power.

The capacitive standoff sensor and the Z actuator are the usual pairing on a gantry flatbed. Other sensors exist. Loop electronics are not shown. Jet shocks, absorption, and gas dynamics are not simulated. Dashed curves show where something goes, not a calculated field.
